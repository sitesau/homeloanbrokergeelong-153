Who this guidance applies to

This angle suits a borrower deciding whether to apply now, adjust the brief or wait. The named scenarios include first home buyers, refinancers, investors, construction borrowers, self-employed borrowers and people considering bridging finance.

  • First home buyers: ask how deposit gaps, lender choice, lenders mortgage insurance, the Victorian First Home Owner Grant and the First Home Guarantee may affect the plan, without assuming eligibility.
  • Refinancers: compare the current rate, fees, offset setup, equity and repayment pressure before deciding whether switching lender is likely to help.
  • Investors: test interest-only, offset, equity-funded deposit and portfolio servicing options before relying on a purchase structure.
  • Builders and bridgers: ask which documents, checkpoints and lender questions need attention before the finance deadline.

Each scenario changes the documents and the timing risk, which is why the discussion should come before the application rather than after.

Broker pathway versus bank pathway

The balanced comparison runs like this. A broker can compare loans from multiple lenders and help identify products that suit the borrower's situation, while a bank can only offer its own loans. Some people still prefer dealing with their existing bank, so the comparison should focus on evidence rather than preference.

Ask what documents are needed, what costs are disclosed, which loan structures are being considered and what the next step would be. For standard residential loans, most residential brokers in Australia are paid commission by the lender and typically do not charge a direct fee. Some brokers may charge a fee in complex situations, such as small loans or unusual circumstances, and should disclose this upfront.

A broker can also help with application paperwork, liaise with the lender and guide the borrower through pre-approval, full approval and settlement. If refinancing or investing later is possible, ask how the current structure may affect future servicing.

Documents, timing and borrowing power

The borrowing power tool asks for income, dependants and existing commitments, then applies the 3% serviceability buffer lenders use. It is an indicative starting point, not a lender-specific approval. Borrowing capacity depends on income, existing debts, living costs, deposit size and property type, and it can vary significantly between banks.

Treat the estimate as a prompt for better questions: what has been verified, what remains assumed, and which lender policies may still change the result? Timing depends on document readiness and lender workload. Straightforward cases with ready documents can sometimes receive pre-approval within a few days, while some lenders may take a week or more. Extra checks, including self-employed income verification, can affect timing, so an approval date should never be relied on until it is confirmed.

  1. Share the pressure point. Explain the goal, the deadline and what is driving the move, whether an inspection weekend, a refinance date or a construction milestone.
  2. Bring the documents. Provide income evidence, existing commitments, deposit details and the suburb or postcode so the discussion can be specific.
  3. Test lender fit. Compare bank, credit union and non-bank options against the situation, including the 3% serviceability buffer estimate.
  4. Apply with fewer surprises. Lodge the application with pre-approval, then full approval and settlement, tracking any extra checks such as self-employed verification.
